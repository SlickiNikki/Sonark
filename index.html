DID YOU KNOW?

Humans kill an estimated 100 million sharks a year (that's 274,000 sharks every day, and 11,000 every hour).

There's over 500 species of sharks ranging from the size of your hand to the size of a school bus.

Over 140 of these are under threat, from vulnerable to critically endangered.

By protecting mid-level fish that eat algae, sharks indirectly help mitigate carbon emissions.

Most sharks reproduce slowly and with around 10-12 years to maturity, shark populations struggle to recover from decline.

THE OCEAN IS FULL OF STORIES

Researchers track sharks to better understand their migrations, behaviours, and the challenges they face in a changing ocean.

Most of these journeys remain hidden from the public. Sonark exists to change that.

SHARKS, WHAT ARE THEY GOOD FOR?

Sharks help keep the ocean ecosystems balanced. As predators, they influence the health of reefs and fish populations.

But many shark species face pressure from overfishing and habitat loss.

Understanding where sharks travel helps researchers protect the places they depend on.

Our Vision

To make shark conservation feel personal.

By turning real tracking data into individual shark stories, Sonark helps more people understand, follow, and care about the animals moving beneath the surface.

Frequently Asked

Are these real sharks?

Yes. Sonark is designed to work with real tracked sharks and conservation partners.

Can I choose my shark?

No. Your shark chooses you.

Each card reveals a unique shark profile, making every Sonark track personal and special.

What do I receive?

A unique physical shark profile card with your shark's name, species, fun facts, conservation status as well as a QR code that connects to its journey through the ocean.

How do I track my shark?

Scan the QR code on your card to open and view your shark's profile in the Sonark app.

There you can see updates, movement history and new Sonark pings.

How often will I get updates?

Updates will depend on your shark's movement and available tracking data.

Some sharks may update you often. Others may disappear for weeks or months before returning.

How does this help conservation?

The goal of Sonark is to support shark research, education, and conservation by turning tracking data into stories people can follow and care about.

What is a ping?

A Sonark ping is an update to your shark's journey.

Updates include a new location, change in movement, new depth, or other significant changes.

What if my shark stops tracking?

This can happen in wildlife tracking.

If a tag stops sending data for an extended period, Sonark can either keep your shark's story archived or connect you with a new shark.
